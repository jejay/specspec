# Review until the next implementation attempt is worth its risk

Review is a spectrum of confidence and attention, not a proposed/accepted workflow
that requires an individual sign-off for every node. Users will often spend more
time understanding and refining earlier nodes near intentions and the manifesto,
and less on later, more easily revisable conclusions. This is a likely allocation
of attention, not a mandatory depth-based score or a claim that a deep choice is
safe. Important or risky details can merit extra review anywhere in the graph.

When the user judges the remaining uncertainty worth risking an implementation
attempt and its token cost, the developed specification is as reviewed as needed
for that attempt. Let implementation and feedback inform the next revision;
proceeding does not make conclusions permanent or prove their correctness.

Keep intentions and conclusions separately addressable through their relationships
and explanations. Do not store proposed/accepted statuses, acceptance scores,
review quotas, or parent-approval gates. Git additions already communicate newness
relative to the selected revision. Changes and linked-source impact provide review
context without imposing an additional approval ceremony.
