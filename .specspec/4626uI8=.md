# Parse editing command chains into one explicit final candidate

Only top-level add, update and delete steps can repeat or combine in one invocation.
Each step keeps its ordinary arguments and options. Bare editing verbs delimit steps;
option values remain literal even when they equal a command name, including both
values of a replacement option. Parse every step
before stdin reads or canonical writes. Reject mixed read/init/recovery/API
commands, unknown/extra arguments and malformed step options without partial edits.

add --as NAME defines an invocation-local new-node alias using one letter followed
by up to 63 letters, digits, underscores or hyphens. Parent $NAME references resolve
to generated IDs, including forward declarations. The complete invocation describes
one final candidate rather than sequential successful operations. Each identity has
one edit; new nodes receive their complete fields in their add step. Reject duplicate
aliases, unknown aliases and conflicting or duplicate node edits. Aliases stay out of
canonical metadata, are scoped to the invocation and reserve no dry-run IDs.

--dry-run, --require-valid, --yes and --json can occur on any editing step and apply
to the whole invocation. A dry run writes no canonical files. Optional validity
checks cover the complete final candidate, independently of step order or initial
invalidity. Combine selected deletions into one assessed consent decision under the
writer lock, preserving exact-file selection, warnings, original bytes and recovery.
The resulting receipt reports generated aliases, changed paths, validity and scope.

Keep single-edit UI argv/stdin and response contracts. Absent messages select bounded stdin body input; one nonempty stream has one
unambiguous step. Reject ambiguous body/replacement sources before writes. Hidden --parents-json and --sources retain
their integration roles. Reviewed deletion plans bind one standalone delete; reject
them in a chain instead of silently discarding their evidence. The sole public editing
surface is add/update/delete. Composed changes use those same steps and field
options. Chains contain at most 1024 steps; argv and aggregate authoring text,
including both sides of replacements, are bounded at 16 MiB in addition to per-node
and smaller operating-system limits.
