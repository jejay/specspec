# Open the agent guide with what the graph is for

The guide begins with its purpose, ahead of every rule. The first paragraph expands the name specification spectrum and describes the graph: a manifesto that states what the product is for, every other node a conclusion drawn from its parents, which are its intentions, the same node an intention for the nodes beneath it, and nodes that run from what the owner intends through ever more concrete conclusions to the files.

The second paragraph gives the reason for the graph. A specification worked out with a coding agent loses track of what was concluded from what. In the graph every conclusion is a node beneath the intentions it was drawn from, one valid conclusion that another can replace. Replacing a node leaves what is above it in force and puts what is beneath it up for review, so the owner can specify one branch in detail, delegate another and change their mind at any depth. The paragraph ends with the agent's part: recording each of its own conclusions as a node under the intention it serves.

A third paragraph gives the agent its first job with anything the owner says: telling intention from conclusion and finding out what the owner holds as intention.

Knowing the purpose lets an agent apply the rules to situations they leave open.
